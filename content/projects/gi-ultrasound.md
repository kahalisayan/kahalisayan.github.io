## Overview

Development of AI methods for gastrointestinal ultrasound, aimed at supporting
clinicians with consistent, reproducible measurements. Topics under study include
bowel-wall segmentation, anatomical boundary detection, bowel-wall thickness
estimation, Doppler vascularity analysis, and grading of disease activity.

## Motivation

Ultrasound is inexpensive, radiation-free, and widely available, but its
interpretation is operator-dependent. Reliable AI could reduce variability in
routine measurements and make quantitative assessment more reproducible across
operators and sessions.

## Research questions

- How can bowel-wall structures be segmented reliably despite operator-dependent
  acquisition and image quality?
- Which measurements are both clinically meaningful and stable enough for automated
  estimation?
- How should uncertainty be surfaced so that automated measurements support, rather
  than replace, clinical judgement?

## Approach

Segmentation and boundary-detection models, quantitative measurement pipelines, and
uncertainty-aware evaluation designed around clinical endpoints rather than
benchmark metrics alone.

## Status

Ongoing and unpublished. This page describes the direction of the work only; it does
not include patient data, proprietary datasets, or internal results.
