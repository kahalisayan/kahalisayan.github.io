## Overview

Quantitative MRI work carried out in the Department of Radiology at Washington
University School of Medicine. The central contribution, DANSE, is a
deep-learning method for accelerated and noise-suppressed estimation of quantitative
Gradient Recalled Echo (qGRE) MRI metrics associated with human-brain neuronal
structure and hemodynamic properties. Related work addresses robust R2*
reconstruction and motion-artifact removal.

## Motivation

Quantitative MRI metrics are sensitive but expensive to acquire cleanly: they suffer
from noise and motion artifacts and require long acquisitions. Learning-based
reconstruction can accelerate acquisition and suppress noise while preserving
quantitative meaning.

## Research questions

- Can qGRE metrics be estimated accurately from accelerated, noisy acquisitions?
- How can biophysical models be integrated with deep learning for robust,
  artifact-free reconstruction?
- How do quantitative metrics relate to neuronal structure, hemodynamics, and
  resting-state functional connectivity?

## Approach

Deep-learning estimation constrained by biophysical models (DANSE), robust and
accelerated reconstruction of R2* images (RoAR), and learning-based motion-artifact
removal (LEARN), alongside analysis of resting-state functional connectivity.
