## Biography

Sayan Kahali is a Senior AI Research Scientist at the Asian Institute of
Gastroenterology Hospitals in Hyderabad, India, where he develops AI methods for
gastrointestinal imaging and clinical decision support. His work sits at the
intersection of biomedical image analysis, medical image segmentation, computer
vision, quantitative MRI, and neuromorphic computing.

Before joining the hospital, he was a Consultant at Tata Consultancy Services
Research & Innovation in Kolkata, where he worked on neuromorphic and efficient AI,
including spiking neural networks for low-power image compression on constrained
edge and satellite systems. From 2019 to 2022 he was a Postdoctoral Research
Associate in the Department of Radiology at Washington University School of Medicine
in St. Louis, working on quantitative MRI reconstruction, motion-artifact removal,
and resting-state functional connectivity. He completed his Ph.D. in Computer
Science and Engineering at Jadavpur University in 2018, with a thesis on volumetric
medical image segmentation supervised by Prof. Jamuna Kanta Sing.

## Research philosophy

The through-line across these areas is a single objective: building AI systems that
are reliable, interpretable, efficient, and clinically meaningful. Methods are only
useful in medicine if they are validated against clinical questions, hold up under
domain shift and noise, and can run within the constraints of the systems that
deploy them. That belief shapes how the work is chosen and evaluated — from
uncertainty-aware prediction in medical imaging to hardware-aware model design for
the edge.
